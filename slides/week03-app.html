Adding transport

How much does transport add?

  • The students have the same constants in front of them on the exercise page; tell them to race you. Type the numbered comments first, then flesh it out with code. Start typing after a minute or so, so the slower ones can follow.

  • Anticipated code:

    # 2. How many *MWh* of *electricity* per tonne of pellets?

# 1 MWh = 3.6 GJ, so a tonne of pellets gives 4.7 MWh of *heat* —
# but only 36% of that is electricity
mwh_per_gj <- 60 * 60 / 1000

mwh_per_tonne <- pellet_gj_per_tonne / mwh_per_gj * plant_efficiency

# 3. kg CO₂ per tonne shipped  
transport_co2 <- 6000 * shipping_co2_per_tkm  # kg CO₂ per tonne

# 4. kg CO₂ per MWh
transport_co2 / mwh_per_tonne                 # kg CO₂ per MWh

  • The answer is ~17.6 kg CO₂/MWh.

  • Do the efficiency step slowly. 17 GJ is heat; only 36% of it is sold as electricity.

  • Ask: “Is that a big number?” Against gas (360) and coal (910) it is small. Against the official figure of zero it is large.

  • Constants match students’ exercise code.

Adding the payback period

Trees absorb CO₂ as they grow. But how long does regrowth take?

  • Fast regrowth (10 years): biomass is roughly carbon-neutral over the short term
  • Slow regrowth (40+ years): biomass has a carbon debt for decades
  • Net zero target: 2050 — only ~20 years away

If the payback exceeds the policy horizon, biomass is not carbon-neutral when it matters.

The same fuel, six answers

Figure 1

w03-payback-scenarios.R

  • Walk left to right. 45 = “Counting all the regrowth that we assume will happen over next 100 yr” Same fuel, same chimney, same megawatt-hour — 1060 kg of difference, all of it from what you decided to count.
  • The two right-hand bars exceed coal(!) Wood (lots of C-O bonds in cellulose – partly oxidized) carries less energy per C than coal (reduced; mostly C).
  • So the case for biomass rests on the regrowth bars on the left. Ask: what would have to be true for the 20-year bar to be the honest one? Who checks that the forest actually regrew? The answer is an assumption, not a measurement.
  • The 20-year bar lands at 365, essentially the same as gas. Worth saying out loud: on a 20-year view, the best case for biomass is “about as good as burning gas.”

Your scenario analysis

✏️💻 Independent exercises

Exercises

The WebR page has four scaffolded exercises:

  1. Transport scenario — estimate CO₂ cost of shipping pellets
  2. Supply chain scenario — add harvesting, processing, drying
  3. Payback scenario — plot cumulative emissions under different regrowth timelines
  4. Extension: Best case and worst case — combine all assumptions

How wide is the range? What does that tell you about certainty?

  • [50 min] Main working block. Circulate. The emission factors and distances are provided in the exercises — students don’t need to research them.
  • Key learning: the answer changes dramatically depending on what you include. The “official” zero is one end of a wide spectrum.

Discussion

💬 What did you find?

Key messages

  • The answer depends on which costs you include
  • The timescale matters — carbon-neutral over centuries, but not over decades
  • The “official” answer (zero) is a policy choice, not a scientific measurement
  • [15 min] Draw these out from the room. Students should feel the force of the ambiguity.
