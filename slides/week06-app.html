t-test workflow

🎓💻 Guided example

The complete workflow

🖥️ Live demo on example data

  1. Load data
  2. Visualise: histograms, boxplots
  3. Check assumptions: shapiro.test(), QQ plot
  4. Run: t.test(y ~ group, data = df)
  5. Interpret: CI, effect size, plain English
  6. If violated: log-transform or wilcox.test()
  • [20 min] Work through the full pipeline on neutral example data. Emphasise: always visualise before you test.

The golden rule

Always visualise before you test.

If you haven’t seen the data, you can’t interpret the result.

Group work

✏️💻 Apply to your data

Your workflow

  1. Load your project data. Make sure it’s clean.
  2. Define your groups / comparison.
  3. Visualise. What does it look like?
  4. Check assumptions.
  5. Run the t-test.
  6. Interpret.
  7. Write up a paragraph of results.

Finished early? Try a second comparison or explore subgroups.

  • [50 min] Groups work on their data. Circulate with demonstrators.
  • Many groups will spend most of this on data cleaning. That’s realistic — say so: “In real research, 80% of the time is data preparation.”
  • If a group’s data don’t suit a t-test (more than 2 groups, categorical variable), help them preview Week 7’s ANOVA.

Discussion

💬 What did you find?

Share your results

2–3 groups share:

  • What was the hypothesis?
  • What was the p-value?
  • What was the effect size?
  • Do you believe the result?
  • [15 min] For each group, model the conditional-thinking habit: “How plausible was this before you tested? Does the p-value change your mind a little or a lot?”

The question to always ask

“How plausible was this before we tested?”

Does the p-value change your mind a little — or a lot?
