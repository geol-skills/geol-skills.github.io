Conflicts?

16:26

Two branches changed the same lines:

Accept Current Change | Accept Incoming Change | Accept Both Changes | Compare Changes
<<<<<<< HEADours · Current
| Ada Lovelace | ada-l | |
=======
| Brian Ripley | brian-r | |
>>>>>>> add-briantheirs · Incoming
  • Ours (Current): the branch you are on
  • Theirs (Incoming): the branch you are merging in
  • Open the file in Positron, click a button (in team.md: Both), save, commit
  • Keeping both is the answer here; elsewhere it is often a hand-edit of the two.
  • Without Positron: delete the three marker lines by hand in any text editor. GitHub Desktop also offers less granular “Use the modified file from …”.

The template

16:45

Draft your plan in research-plan.md

Problem: 1. Research question (one sentence)

Plan:

  1. Hypothesis (what you expect and why)
  2. Data sources (where, what variables, what period)
  3. Comparison / control (what are you comparing to?)
  4. Potential confounders
  5. Analysis plan (what will you test?)
  • [16:45–17:25] Groups draft their plans. Circulate and challenge: “What’s your control?” “How will you handle this confounder?” “Is your sample representative?”
  • “What will you test”: Not “how”, as I’ve not taught you this yet!

Questions to challenge you

17:05

As you draft, we’ll be asking:

  • What’s your control?
  • How will you isolate the effect?
  • Is your sample representative?
  • What assumptions are you making?
  • These should feel familiar by now — they’re the same questions from Weeks 1–4, applied to new topics.

Share your plan

17:25

21 seconds per group:

  1. What’s your question?
  2. What’s your hypothesis?
  3. Where does your data come from?
  4. What’s your comparison?
  • [17:25–17:45] Leave the prompts up while groups prepare; the bar waits, empty. Advance once as the first group starts (back one step to stop it). Then it loops by itself: 21 s to talk, 6 s of grace as it reddens, then a 3 s jingle while the next group gets ready. 30 s a group, so 28 groups take 14 min. Agree the order first (by bench), and have the next group standing.
  • Click the bar to pause it for a comment, again to resume. Save feedback for the end: is the question answerable with the data? Is there a clear comparison? What’s the biggest confounder?
